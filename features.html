Verified, not dismissed

It can see you move.

For push-ups and squats, prop the phone against something and get down. The countdown only runs while the camera can see you moving. Stop, and it pauses. Morning steps are counted live by your phone's pedometer.

  • Nothing is recorded. Frames are reduced to a coarse brightness grid on the phone and discarded immediately.
  • Nothing is uploaded. There's no server to upload to.
  • If the camera can't see you, it hands over to a plain timer rather than trapping you.

The long version: the alarm that makes you exercise.

Habit stacking

Finish one thing, and the next is already waiting.

The best time to build a new habit is right after an existing one, while you're already up and the hard part is done. After three mornings in a row, First Light starts offering you a second mission the moment you finish the first.

  • Say yes once and it's saved: "Push-ups, then Meditation" every morning.
  • Say not now, and it's genuinely not now. It asks again another day.
  • A stacked mission is always optional. It never brings the alarm back.

Why it works: habit stacking, explained.

Progress

Watch it climb.

Streaks, mornings won, and the numbers you actually logged, charted over weeks. Your level is simply the count of mornings you've won, and it never resets.

  • The streak respects your schedule. Weekday alarms don't break it over a weekend.
  • A missed morning costs you a streak, not your progress. Consistency is measured in months, not days.
  • Ranks land on the habit-science milestones: seven days, thirty, sixty-six.

Where 66 comes from: how long a habit really takes.

Privacy

Everything stays on your phone.

No accounts. No server. No analytics.

  • Your photos, sessions and streak live in the app's private storage and are deleted with it.
  • Camera frames are processed on the phone in real time and never stored.
  • No third-party code, no trackers, no advertising.
  • Payment goes through Apple. We never see your card.
Missing one day doesn't undo a habit. Researchers found a single miss has no measurable effect. Two is a pattern.
